# Questions about: A daily short-pick report per picker so the shift leads can target retraining

Please answer what you can, in your own words. If you don't know, say so. That's a useful answer.
Real examples help most: the last time it happened, what you saw, who was involved.

1. **What will you do differently when you see this report?**
   _Why: A report nobody acts on isn't worth building; the decision is what it's for._  
   _For example: "Decide which suppliers to call about late deliveries that week."_

2. **Who's involved: who does the work today, who uses the result, who owns the process, and who signs off on changes?**
   _Why: Knowing who uses it and who can say yes keeps the build pointed at the right people._  
   _For example: "A coordinator pulls it together today, three team leads use it, and the operations manager signs off on changes."_

3. **How would you know this worked? What's it costing today, in hours, errors or money, and what would good look like?**
   _Why: A number for today and a target are what turn "better" into something you can check._  
   _For example: "Today nobody can see it until month end. It would be working if the leads saw it each morning and acted on it."_

4. **Where does the information come from, which copy do you trust, and is any of it sensitive?**
   _Why: The build has to read from the right source, and sensitive data changes what's allowed._  
   _For example: "It comes from our main system's reporting copy; that's the one we trust. No customer or pricing data."_

5. **Which screens, systems or files do you use for this, and is anything changing in them in the next six months?**
   _Why: The systems decide how it can be built, and an upcoming change can make a build wasted._  
   _For example: "Our main system and a spreadsheet. There's an upgrade planned for next year."_

6. **Are there rules it must follow: policies, deadlines, time windows, approvals, or anyone outside your team who requires it?**
   _Why: Constraints rule options in or out before anyone starts building._  
   _For example: "It has to be ready before the morning meeting. Nothing outside the team requires it."_

7. **When it goes wrong, who catches it today, how bad is a wrong result, and what would you do if the new thing stopped working?**
   _Why: This sets how much checking a person must do, and what the fallback is._  
   _For example: "A team lead spots odd numbers. A wrong number means acting on the wrong thing. If it broke, we'd go back to the spreadsheet."_

8. **How exactly is one counted? What's in and what's out?**
   _Why: Two people counting "the same thing" differently is the most common reason reports get distrusted._  
   _For example: "A late delivery is anything after the agreed date. Cancelled orders don't count."_

9. **Is there a report or screen today that's close to this? What's missing from it?**
   _Why: If something already covers it, the answer might be to fix that, not to build new._  
   _For example: "The system's dashboard shows totals, but nothing per supplier."_

10. **Is any part of this done by hand today, like a spreadsheet you build?**
   _Why: Work done by hand today is often worth fixing first, or is what the new thing would replace._  
   _For example: "Yes: every Monday I rebuild the sheet from an export, about four hours."_

11. **What would make you stop this, or switch it off?**
   _Why: Agreeing up front when to stop protects everyone if it doesn't work out._  
   _For example: "If its numbers disagreed with the WMS two weeks running, we'd switch it off."_
